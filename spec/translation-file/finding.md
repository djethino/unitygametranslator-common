# Finding the links in a text nobody typed — the written rules

When a model answers, a retranslation is accepted, or a cold translation is read in, the text is clear text (no marker).
`LinkFinding.Find(index, text, source, before)` returns `v` with its `[!g*N]`, the `links`, and the links a person had
set that cannot be found again. Companion of `editing.md` (§6) and `casing.md`. Held by `corpus/rules/link_finding.json`
and `word_glue.json`. Only the mod and the Manager use it (`manifest.json`, `held_by`): no port in another language.

## 1. The principle

**A link is known, never guessed** (R4). A term is a candidate for a sentence only if the SOURCE of the sentence cites it;
its known writings are then looked for in the text; ONE occurrence is a link, none or several is no link. Nothing is
ever rewritten — the text rendered is the text received. Choosing the form belongs to the model; the program reads the
choice it made.

## 2. The index

`TermIndex.Build(lines)` — once per file, immutable. The terms are the lines that carry the term mark (`term: true`) and
words (a template key, or a term with no `v`, is none). A term's writings are its `v` and its forms, each with the first
letter in both cases (`Casing`). A term is numbered by the ORDINAL order of its key: nothing depends on the order the file
lists its terms in. A sentence costs the length of its text, not the number of terms (a trie over the keys and one over the
writings); `LinkFindingChecks` measures it.

## 3. What the source cites

The key of a term, found in the source at word edges (§5), the first letter in either case; **the longest first, never
overlapping** (« Star Coin » before « Coin »); ties by the ordinal order of the keys. A term the line refuses
(`unlinked`) is never proposed — its place in the source is still taken. The source of a term's own line, and a template,
carry no link.

**No ending is allowed.** « Star Coins » does not cite « Star Coin »: a plural of the source is cited only if the file has a
term for it. The prototype allowed an ending up to half the word; measured on recorded answers, that made one game go
from 1 205 to 2 347 cited pairs and another from 449 to 504 (a constant, and it cites too much).

## 4. What the text writes

For each cited term, each of its writings is looked for in the text at word edges, the first letter in either case; the
form is the one found (`null` for `v`), the `case` is set when the first letter differs from the writing's. Places are
taken the longest first (then the key, the form, the place), never overlapping: a shorter writing inside a longer one finds
its own occurrence elsewhere, or none. A term with **exactly one** place is a link (`by: A`, `was` = the words found); none
or several is none.

*A short writing inside a compound word of the text* (« touche » in « carte-touche »): the hyphen is no letter, so it is an
occurrence; found once it is a link, in colour, and the person can unlink it. We do not try to be cleverer.

## 5. Where a word ends

An edge cuts a word when the character inside it and the one outside it are **both** written stuck together into words —
Unicode's line breaking classes AI AK AL AP AS CM H2 H3 HL JL JT JV NU VF VI ZWJ (`WordGlue.Classes`): the letters of a
script that spaces its words, digits, the marks and viramas that belong to a letter. Everything else is an edge: spaces
(the no-break one too), punctuation, the hyphen, the apostrophe (« d'étoile »), and every position between ideographs,
kana, Thai, Lao, Khmer, Myanmar (classes ID, CJ, SA …), which write no space. A combining mark after the last letter is
part of the word. A digit stuck to a letter is the same word.

The table (`WordGlue.Tables.g.cs`) is generated by `tools/generate-word-glue.py` from `LineBreak.txt` of the project's
pinned Unicode version (`WordGlue.UnicodeVersion`, equal to the mod's and to `Casing.UnicodeVersion`; the file's SHA-256 is
in the header; `--check` says whether the committed table is the one the source gives). It is the SAME data as the mod's
`ShapingTables.LineBreakClasses` read as a yes/no: a code point is glued exactly when its class there is one of
`WordGlue.Classes` (checked at the time of writing: 43 735 characters, equal). The mod may read the common table instead
of keeping its own copy of this reading; its full class table stays what it is for line breaking.

## 6. The links a person set

`before` holds the line as it was. A link `by: H` is kept — as it is — when its exact words (`own` if it has one, else
`was`) are found **once** in the text, at word edges, as many places as links with the same words (paired in reading order);
otherwise it is named in `NotFound`, to be shown BEFORE she accepts the text (accepting drops it). A term she linked is not
linked again by the machine in that sentence. What she refused is not proposed.

## 7. Limits, said

- A plural of the source is not cited unless the file has a term for it (§3).
- A term written the same way as another, both cited: the place goes to the first key in the ordinal order; the other has
  no place left.
- A term with two occurrences of its writings gets no link even when one of them is the word of another construction.
- The text must be clear: a link marker in it is refused (`text-has-link-marker`).
- A script with no spaces is found by containment, the longest first.

## 8. For a port: « does this source cite this term, and where » (`LinkFinding.Cites`)

The editors of the site must say, in the window of a term, « this text cites Star Coin and the translation does not write it »,
and, when a term is created, « the other texts that cite this word and what they say today ». That is the first half of
`Find` (§3), alone: **the candidate half**. A port does NOT port `Find`; it ports `cites`, held by
`corpus/rules/link_finding.json` (`cites`, under `sides.editor`).

`cites(terms, source, unlinked)` → the places `[{term, start, end}]` where the source cites a key of `terms`:

- the key found in the source at word edges (§5), the **first letter in either case** (`Casing`: the capital by the table
  `corpus/data/first-letter.json`);
- **the longest first, never overlapping**; at equal length the first key in the ORDINAL order of the keys (UTF-16 code
  units, as the hash's keys); a term cited twice is two places;
- a term in `unlinked` (the line refuses it) is never proposed, **its place is still taken** (« Coin » inside a refused
  « Star Coin » is not cited either);
- the source of a term's own line (it IS a key of `terms`) and a template (it holds `[!L*`) cite nothing;
- positions are **UTF-16 code units**, `[start, end)`; **no ending is allowed** (§3).

**What a port must read, and not its engine**: where a word ends is `corpus/data/word-glue.json` — the characters written
stuck together into words, 847 inclusive ranges of code points as a flat `[first, last, …]` array (10 254 bytes, about
4 kB compressed), generated by `tools/generate-word-glue.py` from the project's Unicode version (`--check`, SHA of the source
in the file, held equal to the C# table over every code point by `CitesBenchChecks`). An edge cuts a word only when the code
point inside it and the one outside it are BOTH in the table (`edge_before`, `edge_after`, `is_glued` of `word_glue.json`, also
under `sides.editor`). A browser's `\p{L}` or `\b` is the browser's Unicode, not the project's: a port never asks it.

**How a port must go about it — an index, once** (the principle of `TermIndex`, §2). A file of 100 000 lines and 5 000 terms must
not test every sentence against every term at every rendering: that is 5·10⁸ searches (the Python reference below, naive, makes
9.7 million in about a second and a half for a tenth of that file). The port builds, ONCE per file (or when a term's key
changes), a trie over the keys — each key, and its first letter in the other case, as the C# does — and reads each source
left to right, trying a match only where `edge_before` allows a word to begin and keeping the ends `edge_after` allows. A source
then costs its own length, not the number of terms. Measured in C#: 24 ms and 25 MB to index 5 000 terms (`TermIndex`, keys and
writings); 17 µs and 3 kB per sentence at 5 000 terms, the same as at 50; **6 000 sources answered in 31 ms over 1 620 terms
indexed in 2 ms** (`corpus/data/cites-bench.json`). The index is rebuilt when the set of keys changes, never per sentence;
for the sources of a whole file, answer them once and keep the places, and ask again only for a sentence that was edited.

**The file that makes a naive port feel it**: `corpus/data/cites-bench.json` — 1 620 terms, 6 000 sources (366 kB, 138 kB
compressed), 25 539 places — with `result_sha256`, the SHA-256 of the canonical answer (for each source in order and each
place in reading order, `source-index TAB key TAB start TAB end LF`, positions in UTF-16 code units, no refused term). It was
made by a **naive reference written in Python only from these rules** (`tools/generate-cites-bench.py`, which reads the two data
files and never the C#); the C# index gives the same 25 539 places, to the position. A port holds both: the same sha, and a
time it chooses and measures itself — the corpus states no limit, because a limit is a machine's.

## 9. For a port: « where is THIS term written in THIS text » (`LinkFinding.WrittenIn`)

The second half of `Find`, for ONE term, every place kept — what the term window shows for each line that cites the term
(§8): what the term REPLACES in the translation, by hand, as the smart optimisation would. Held by `corpus/rules/link_finding.json`
(`written_in`, under `sides.editor`).

`written_in(text, writings, claimed)` → `[{writing, start, end, case}]`:

- `text` is the text SHOWN of the line (`EditState.Text` of `LinkEditing.Show`: no marker), in clear; a text that holds a link
  marker answers nothing.
- `writings` are the term's writings **in this order**: its own word (form `null`) first, then its forms in the ordinal order of their
  ids (the order `Find` numbers them in), then any CANDIDATE the person has just selected and that is not yet a form. A candidate is a
  writing like the others — the caller remembers which is which by the position (`writing` in the answer). A writing with no words, with
  a marker in it, or with the words of an earlier one has no place of its own.
- `claimed` are the spans `[start, end)` of the passages the line already links (`EditState.Passages`: `Start`, `Start + Length`):
  a place that overlaps one is not offered.
- Each writing is looked for at word edges (§5, `corpus/data/word-glue.json`), its first letter in either case
  (`corpus/data/first-letter.json`); `case` says which was applied (`upper-first`, `lower-first`) or is `null` when the text holds the
  words as they are.
- The places are taken **the longest first**; at an equal length **a writing found as it is beats another writing's capital landing on
  the same words**, then the first writing given; never overlapping each other nor a claimed span. **All of them are returned**, in
  reading order: **one** place is what `Find` would link, **several** are the person's to choose (« this text writes it twice »), **none**
  is « not found ».

**It agrees with `Find` — one rule, two doors.** `WrittenInChecks` (1 500 random texts, fixed seed) holds it: where `written_in` gives one place,
`Find` over the same term links THAT place with the same form, the same case and the same words; where it gives none or several, `Find`
links nothing. (The tie above is the one place the two disagreed — a capital of « coin » landing on the form « Coin »; `Find` now takes
the writing found as it is, like `LinkTo`.) Two things `Find` does that this does not: it looks only for the terms the source cites, jointly
and longest-first ACROSS terms (here one term has the text to itself — the screen must not offer a place a longer word of another term has),
and it keeps only the single place.

**The line is written by `LinkEditing.LinkTo`, with no new case.** To link the places the person kept: `Show` the line, then one
`LinkTo(state, start, end - start, term, null, …)` per place, in any order, with the places of THIS answer (the text does not change when no
form is given, and the places do not overlap, so none is refused with `already-a-term`). With no form given `LinkTo` finds the writing
by its words — exact first, then the capitals — and writes the same form and the same case the place announced (`WrittenInChecks` holds it,
on lines that already hold a link too). A candidate writing becomes a form of the term BEFORE it is linked, by the term's edit (the entry
`entryOf` answers is the term as it WOULD be, new form included); a selection that is not a known writing is the usual question of changed
words, not a refusal. Several links on one line are several calls; a form created and linked in one gesture is the form's creation (the
term's edit) and then the link.

**What it costs.** C#: 1 000 lines of about sixty letters with ten writings, 6 ms in all (6 µs a line); a line costs its length times
the number of writings (a native `indexOf` per writing and per case, and a word-edge test at each hit). A port should: call it only for
the lines `cites` (§8) returned for the term — never the whole file; ask again when the term's writings change (a new word, a form added or
changed, a candidate selected) — an event, never on a timer; compute it for the rows the window shows and keep the answer per line until
the writings or the line change; and not build an index (a few writings do not need one). A few hundred lines at each change is a few
milliseconds.
